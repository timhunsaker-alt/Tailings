# Heavy Iron fixes (branch `heavy-iron-fixes`, 2026-11-22)

Decided while Tim was away, on the best recommendation; each is open for review. The weak spots come from the series plan
(`videos/longform/heavy-iron/PLAN.md`, the "Weak" notes for episodes 3, 4, 5 and 7). Format as in [README.md](README.md).

## Fix 1: the blast (episode 3)

### 1. Every hole throws fragments, not only a bad one
- **Chosen:** each firing hole throws 13 drawn fragments (8 off its face, 5 out of its collar) that fly, strike and land as dust; a bad
  hole's flyrock blocks (blowout 6, short burden 3, overcharge 3) are kept as they were and are now drawn in the air too.
- **Why:** real blasts throw some rock from every hole; the episode needs to show it. Fragments land as dust, not blocks, so a good
  shot does not litter the pit with muck 100 m away (P3 decision 12 kept: only a bad hole's flyrock lands as muck).
- **Alternatives:** every fragment landing as a muck block (litter on every shot); fragments only from bad holes (nothing to film).

### 2. The reach follows Richards and Moore, with k = 13.5
- **Chosen:** face burst L = k²/g (√m/B)^2.6 and cratering L = k²/g (√m/SH)^2.6 (Richards and Moore 2004), k = 13.5, each held to
  the game's 100 m. The default hole (69 kg/m, 4 m of stemming) reaches the 100 m cap; 6 m of stemming 43 m, 8 m 20 m.
- **Why:** it is the rule real blasters use to size a clearance; it is scaled by the charge a metre, the burden (so the powder factor)
  and the stemming, as asked. k's fitted range (13.5-27) is *unverified* here; the low end keeps a well-stemmed shot inside the cap.
- **Alternatives:** k = 27 (every default shot at the cap whatever the stemming); a made-up linear scale on powder factor.

### 3. Anyone in the open within reach is struck once a shot
- **Chosen:** as each hole fires, one fragment is aimed at each player or mob standing in the open within that hole's ballistic reach
  (the face's speed within 60 degrees in front of it, the collar's any way); it hits for 10 (falling-block damage: a helmet counts),
  once a shot whoever fires first. A roof over the head stops it. Random fragments also hit what they cross.
- **Why:** so clearing the zone, or taking cover, matters every time, and the game tests can measure it; a real clearance treats any
  point within flyrock range as struck. *Needs Tim:* this is harsher than chance; a 20 hp villager 90 m behind a default shot loses
  half its health. Cows in the zone die.
- **Alternatives:** chance only (a player at 80 m almost never hit, so the siren would not matter); a chance falling with distance.

### 4. The danger zone, the firing point and the siren
- **Chosen:** ARM reads "flyrock to N m: clear N m" (the zone is the farther of the flyrock reach and the blast's 50 m) and warns when
  the firing point is inside it; FIRE's player check uses the zone; through the siren each player in the zone gets a countdown and
  "clear the zone or take cover" each second. The firing point's 50 m minimum is unchanged.
- **Why:** the siren and clearing now have a figure behind them. Keeping the 50 m firing point and telling the blaster to fire from
  cover is real practice (a blast shelter).
- **Alternatives:** a firing point held outside the flyrock reach (100 m for a default shot; the lead line allows 500 m).

### 5. The muck pile slumps to its angle of repose
- **Chosen:** after the last hole the broken rock rolls off its face by the settle rule (37 degrees), top down, a few thousand cells a
  tick; the swell still spills off the face.
- **Why:** the box of muck stood as a vertical wall, which reads as unbroken rock on camera. The throw forward (real piles run out one
  to two bench heights) is not modelled: it would move most of the shot and change where a shovel finds it.
- **Alternatives:** throwing the front rows' muck forward (a bigger change to where the muck is); a lumpy muck model (thousands of
  non-full blocks cost frames).

## Fix 2: the dozer (episode 4)

### 6. Tracks that run, drawn as sliding grousers
- **Chosen:** a row of steel grousers along the top and ground runs of each track, each run its own model group, slid a 0.25 m pitch
  and round again with the distance rolled (`TrackDozerModel.shoeShift`); the model and skin are regenerated by
  `tools/models/pit_vehicles.py` (the skin repacked, the same Ludo materials).
- **Why:** an entity model cannot scroll a texture; sliding lugs by the rolled distance shows the track running at its real speed.
- **Alternatives:** a link-by-link chain round the idler and sprocket (many more boxes for a few pixels).

### 7. Pitched and rolled on the ground, the dozer only
- **Chosen:** the renderer samples the ground under the front and rear, left and right of the tracks (the highest there) and pitches
  and rolls the body, eased over a few frames; only the track dozer. Its collision box stays level.
- **Why:** the plan asks for the dozer; the trucks and shovels keep P1's level drawing (ROADMAP). *Needs Tim:* the same could be turned
  on for every pit machine.
- **Alternatives:** tilting the box too (collision against a tilted box is a far bigger change).

### 8. Spreading as a third CARRY setting; no compaction
- **Chosen:** the sprint key in CARRY steps berm 1 m, berm 2 m, SPREAD. In SPREAD a loaded blade fills the open ground its tracks stand
  on ahead of it, a block a column a metre, so its load becomes a 1 m lift it drives on along. Compaction is left out.
- **Why:** real dry stacks are spread in lifts and compacted; a block is the thinnest lift. Compaction has nothing to act on (no density
  or stability for a block, dry tails a plain falling block); a pass counter with no effect would be show only. *Needs Tim:* say if a
  compacted state (firmer, not pushed by the blade) is wanted.
- **Alternatives:** a SPREAD blade position (a fourth jump step); a compaction pass count on a new dry-tails block state.

## Fix 3: the TBM (episode 5)

### 9. Cut square, backfilled round
- **Chosen:** the face is still cut 8 x 8 (the cutterhead and erector are 8 x 8 parts); behind the erector, as the ring goes in, the
  twelve corner cells outside an 8 m circle get their own rock back, or cobblestone grout where they held ore, soil, air or water.
- **Why:** a round cut would collide with the square parts and their set-down; a shield TBM really does leave an annulus that is grouted
  behind the segments. The bored tunnel reads round from inside and behind the machine.
- **Alternatives:** round parts (new multiblocks and set-down rules); a larger 10 m circle around the 6 x 6 clear (a new geometry for
  every drive, floor and gantry).

### 10. The ring is 18 segments
- **Chosen:** the ring is the circle's edge, 18 cells, so a ring costs 18 crafted segments or 18 rock (was 28); a stone advance mucks
  48, not 60. Worlds bored before keep their square tunnels; a ring waiting at save time is placed with grout at its corners.
- **Why:** the lining follows the round bore. *Needs Tim:* fewer segments a ring makes the drive cheaper.
- **Alternatives:** keep charging 28 and place 18 (wasteful and odd).

### 11. The head drawn round, the wheel always shown
- **Chosen:** the running machine skips its cutterhead and tail-shield cells outside the circle, and the wheel is drawn on the face at
  all times, turning only while it cuts. Parked as blocks it is square.
- **Why:** the face read square with a wheel drawn over it only while cutting. The airflow keeps its 36 m² (a 6.8 m bore's area).
- **Alternatives:** round block models for the parts as placed (a part model change, left for an art wave).

## Fix 4: the shaft (episode 7)

### 12. The cage first; the raise borer left for its own pass
- **Chosen:** the cage is built; the raise borer (shaft-and-hoist.md L4b: a 4 x 6 x 6 part, a pilot and a ream a layer a minute, its
  cuttings into the drive, lining, a 400 kW motor) is not.
- **Why:** the plan asks for the cage first if both do not fit one pass; the raise borer is a new multiblock with its own stage, model
  and tests. *Needs Tim:* the raise borer as its own wave; episode 7 says the shaft was dug before filming.

### 13. Its own compartment, the same hoist, one at a time
- **Chosen:** the cage hangs beside the skips (x 3-4, z 5-6), on the hoist house's motor; it moves only with the skips at rest and the
  skips wait for it. It does not replace a skip (the spec's CAGE mode).
- **Why:** "beside the skips", as asked: the skips keep their balance and the ore keeps coming between trips. A real mine would often
  give the cage its own service hoist with a counterweight. *Needs Tim:* a separate service hoist house.
- **Alternatives:** the spec's CAGE mode (a skip off its rope while people ride); a second hoist house.

### 14. 4 people, 9 stacks, 4 m/s, a 5 s bell
- **Chosen:** the spec's 2 x 3 x 2 cage for 4 people and its man-riding 4 m/s; 9 stacks on its deck; a 5 s bell before each trip; the
  skips' acceleration and creep.
- **Why:** the spec's proposals; real cages carry more people and wind faster in deep shafts (all *proposed*).

### 15. Calls without new blocks
- **Chosen:** boarding rings to go to the other landing; sneaking with an empty hand on the headframe calls it up, on the flask down.
  A gate holds riders in while it moves (they are put back aboard).
- **Why:** the spec's `shaft_call` blocks would add a block, model and art for what two existing parts can do.
- **Alternatives:** `shaft_call` blocks at each landing; PLC points for the cage.

## The raise borer (follow-up, the coordinator's request)

### 16. As L4b planned: a pilot down, a ream up, at the spec's scaled rates
- **Chosen:** the spec's 4 x 6 x 6 part, 400 kW, 200 m look-down for a drive under 3 m of rock, a metre of pilot each 10 s and a metre
  of ream each 60 s, a 5 m (5 x 5) raise.
- **Why:** the spec's figures (all *proposed*; real reaming makes a few metres a day).

### 17. The raise stops under the collar slab
- **Chosen:** the ream stops at the layer under the borer's pad; that metre is left for the player to take out after moving the borer.
- **Why:** the borer stands on it; a real raise is finished under the machine's pad once it is moved.
- **Alternatives:** reaming through and leaving the borer hanging over the hole.

### 18. Unlined, grouted where open
- **Chosen:** no lining; an open or flooded cell round a reamed layer is grouted with cobblestone.
- **Why:** raise-bored shafts in sound rock usually stand unlined *(unverified)*; lining 5 x 5 walls a metre a minute would cost a stack
  of segments every few minutes. *Needs Tim:* the spec said lining as the TBM's.

### 19. Where the raise lies, and the headframe after
- **Chosen:** the 5 x 5 sits at x 0-4, z 1-5 in the borer's frame (centred on its derrick); the headframe's shaft (x 2-6, z 2-6 in its
  frame) is lined up over it by the player after the borer is taken off.
- **Alternatives:** the borer reading where a headframe would stand (no headframe exists yet when it bores).

## Weak on camera, left as is
- Fragments are small at a long shot's distance; film the blast from 60-80 m.
- No camera shake; the blast sound is still vanilla's explosion.
- `pit_dozer_cut` and other known intermittent scenes are untouched.
